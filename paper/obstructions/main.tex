\begin{abstract}
We study which standard methods cannot prove the lonely runner inequality $\ML(v)\ge 1/(m+1)$ for positive integer speed vectors, and why. (i) At the target level, no integrable weight certifies a vector with $\ML(v)\le1/(m+1)$, so first-moment and Riesz-product certificates are blind to tight vectors; this is a formalized restatement of a lemma of Bedert. (ii) Five explicit families with $12$ to $18$ speeds defeat bounded-level base-point certificates of the kinds tested, and without a leave-one-out hypothesis no bound on denominators of lonely times depending only on $m$ exists. (iii) For tight vectors of Goddyn--Wong type, $m+1$ divides no speed; a conjecture (T) extends this to all tight primitive vectors, and at a maximiser of a tight vector the denominator is $m+1$ times a divisor of the gcd of a corner pair. (iv) The doubling graph on the non-units in the window $[1,2m]$ has an isolated vertex whenever $2m+1$ is composite and exceeds $9$, hence no perfect matching. (v) Covering-radius bounds at the target value are false. (vi) An inequality $\min_v\ML(V\setminus v)\le F(\ML(V))$ with $F$ continuous at the floor cannot send counterexamples to tight subsets: at $11$ values of $m$, exact tight vectors have all leave-one-out values above the threshold that $F$ would have to respect (HEURISTIC, finite exact computation). The leave-one-out values of $70$ known tight vectors are tabulated and a general lower bound is conjectured. (vii) The bad region has the first homology of the torus for $m\ge2$ and its fundamental group for $m\ge3$, so topological necessary conditions obtained from the inclusion are satisfied by every speed vector with not all speeds odd (the topological statements are PROVED; that this delimits a method is HEURISTIC). Each statement carries a label; attributions to prior work are listed in Section~\ref{sec:attr}. No progress on the conjecture is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Setting}
A speed vector is $v=(v_1,\dots,v_m)\in\Z_{>0}^m$; there are $m+1$ runners, one of them stationary. Write $\norm{x}=\operatorname{dist}(x,\Z)$, $\gap_v(t)=\min_i\norm{v_it}$ and $\ML(v)=\max_{t\in\R}\gap_v(t)$ (a maximum, since $\gap_v$ is continuous and $1$-periodic). The lonely runner conjecture (LRC) states $\ML(v)\ge1/(m+1)$ for all $v$. A vector is \emph{tight} if $\ML(v)=1/(m+1)$; $(1,\dots,m)$ is tight. The bad set of speed $i$ at level $\delta$ is $B_i=\{t:\norm{v_it}<\delta\}$ and the good set is $G_\delta=\{t:\forall i,\ \norm{v_it}\ge\delta\}$; a time $t$ is \emph{lonely at level $\delta$} if $t\in G_\delta$, that is if $\gap_v(t)\ge\delta$. A vector is \emph{divisor-complete} (DC) if every $j\in\{2,\dots,m+1\}$ divides some $v_i$. Throughout, $m$ is the number of speeds; the literature cited uses $n$ for $m$ or for $m+1$, and each citation below is translated.

The inequality is known for $m\le 12$ (Rosenfeld for eight runners \cite{Rosenfeld25}; Trakulthongchai for nine and ten \cite{Trak26}, refereed; Sungkawichai and Trakulthongchai up to thirteen runners \cite{ST26}, unrefereed; Allikvere claims fourteen and fifteen runners \cite{Allik26}, unrefereed, computer-assisted). The generic bounds are $1/(2m)$ by a union bound (\cite[Prop.~1.1]{Tao18}, \cite[Section~5]{PS25}) and $1/(2m)+m^{-5/3-o(1)}$ \cite[Thm.~1.3]{Bedert25}.

The reduction studied in the accompanying archive reduces the conjecture for all $m$, given three finite statements, to the statement \texttt{FloorCruxU}: \emph{if the floor holds for all smaller numbers of speeds, then for $m\ge12$ every primitive $v$ such that every leave-one-out subvector is primitive, $v$ is DC, $v$ admits a relation $\sum c_iv_i=0$ with $c\in\{-1,0,1\}^m\setminus\{0\}$, and no Riesz-product certificate (Section~\ref{sec:measure}) fires, satisfies $\ML(v)\ge1/(m+1)$} (Lean: \texttt{FloorCruxU}, \texttt{universalFloor\_of\_four\_atoms\_U}). This statement is open. The methods below are those that were aimed at it.

\subsection{Conceptual abstract}
The note tests seven standard routes to the floor at tight vectors. A fixed integrable weight cannot certify a tight vector: its good set is countable and without mass. Bounded-denominator certificates fail without a leave-one-out hypothesis, because a speed divisible by $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,Q)$ vanishes at every time of denominator at most $Q$. Doubling pairings fail because, for odd composite $2m+1$ above $9$, the non-unit $2m+1-2p$, with $p$ the least prime factor, has no partner in the window. Corner analysis fails because a corner maximum depends on the residues of all other speeds. Covering-radius bounds fail because the shifted statement is false. Descent through leave-one-out subsets fails because some tight vectors have every leave-one-out value above the next value attained by $m-1$ speeds. Topological conditions fail because the bad region has the first homology of the torus. What remains is an explicit rational time or a classification of tight vectors.

\section{Volume and smooth-weight certificates}\label{sec:measure}

For a nonnegative $F\in L^1[0,1]$ and a level $\delta$ consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
\sum_i\int_{[0,1]\cap B_i}F<\int_{[0,1]}F .
\end{equation}
If $\ML(v)<\delta$, every $t$ lies in some $B_i$, so $F\le\sum_i\mathbf 1_{B_i}F$ pointwise and \eqref{eq:cert} fails. The weights $F=P^D$, $P(t)=\prod_i(1-\cos2\pi v_it)$, are Riesz products; $U_D$ \emph{fires} if \eqref{eq:cert} holds for $F=P^D$ at $\delta=1/(m+1)$. The case $F\equiv1$ is the union bound of \cite[Prop.~1.1]{Tao18}. For an arbitrary probability measure $\mu$ and closed bad sets the inequality $\int\Phi\,d\mu\ge1$, $\Phi=\sum_i\mathbf 1_{\{\norm{v_i x}\le\delta\}}$, at $\delta=\ML(v)$ is \cite[Lemma~2.1]{Bedert25}.

\begin{lemma}[Level sets]\label{lem:level}
For $a\in\Z\setminus\{0\}$ and $\delta\in\R$, $\{t:\norm{at}=\delta\}\subset\{(n\pm\delta)/a:n\in\Z\}$ is countable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:barrier}
Let $v\in(\Z\setminus\{0\})^m$, $\delta\in\R$ and $\ML(v)\le\delta$. Then $G_\delta$ is countable, hence Lebesgue-null. Consequently, for every measurable $F\ge0$ integrable on $[0,1]$,
\[\int_{[0,1]}F\le\sum_i\int_{[0,1]\cap B_i}F,\]
so \eqref{eq:cert} implies $\delta<\ML(v)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $t\in G_\delta$. Then $\gap_v(t)\ge\delta\ge\ML(v)\ge\gap_v(t)$, so $\gap_v(t)=\delta$. As $\gap_v$ is a minimum over finitely many terms, some $i$ has $\norm{v_it}=\delta$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:level}, $G_\delta$ is a finite union of countable sets. Outside $G_\delta$ some $i$ has $t\in B_i$, hence $F(t)\le\sum_i\mathbf 1_{B_i}(t)F(t)$ for almost every $t$; integrate over $[0,1]$.
\end{proof}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over (\texttt{goodSet\_countable}, \texttt{goodSet\_null}, \texttt{integral\_le\_sum\_badSet}, \texttt{lt\_ML\_of\_integral\_unionBound}). The inequality for absolutely continuous measures is Bedert's Lemma~2.1 read with $\mu=F\,dt$; the countability of $G_\delta$ is elementary}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:tight}
If $\ML(v)\le1/(m+1)$, in particular if $v$ is tight, no $U_D$ fires, $D\ge0$. Conversely, if $U_D$ fires then $1/(m+1)<\ML(v)$.
\end{corollary}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over (\texttt{not\_UDFires\_of\_tight}, \texttt{lt\_ML\_of\_UDFires}); the hypothesis is not vacuous, $\ML(1,\dots,m)\le1/(m+1)$ (\texttt{ML\_consec\_le}, \texttt{not\_UDFires\_consec}). The corollary is an immediate consequence of the cited lemma of Bedert}

\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} concerns \eqref{eq:cert} at one fixed level and one fixed weight. It does not exclude a weight $F_{\delta'}$ chosen separately for each level $\delta'<\ML(v)$: the indicator of $\{\gap_v>\delta'\}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:cert} exactly for $\delta'<\ML(v)$, but constructing it requires knowing the lonely times. What is excluded is a weight given by a formula uniform in $v$, such as $P^D$, at the target level. Atomic measures are outside the theorem: a point mass at a rational time is the escape lemma (Proposition~\ref{prop:escape}), and the uniform measure on $\{j/p\}$ with $p$ a prime dividing no speed is \cite[Lemma~5.3, Cor.~5.4]{Bedert25}. Volume comparisons of geometry-of-numbers type (successive minima, covering radii bounded through volume) were not shown to reduce to \eqref{eq:cert}; the claim that they do is not made. Label: HEURISTIC for the scope statements, as they delimit methods and are not theorems.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Obstruction for volume methods.} A first-moment argument that uses only the measures of the individual bad sets gives $1/(2m)$, because each $B_i$ has measure $2\delta$ and the sum must reach $1$; the factor two is the multiplicity with which bad sets overlap, which this argument discards. Sharper volume arguments must control the intersections $B_i\cap B_j$, and these depend on the starting phases of the runners, whereas the individual measures do not; since the shifted statement is false (Section~\ref{sec:phase}), a volume argument can pass the exponent only by using the zero phase through the intersection pattern. At the target value itself the pattern is rigid: by Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} the bad sets of a tight vector cover the circle up to a null set, so a weight can gain nothing beyond equality.

\paragraph{Obstruction for smooth-weight certificates.} A Riesz product or any other integrable weight turns the existence of a lonely time into a strict inequality between two Lebesgue integrals, and at a vector with $\ML(v)=1/(m+1)$ the two integrals are forced by Theorem~\ref{thm:barrier} to satisfy the opposite non-strict inequality, because the lonely set is countable and carries no mass. The mechanism is that $\gap_v$ is a minimum of finitely many tent functions, each of whose level sets is discrete; no choice of weight can see a set of measure zero. The obstruction persists off the tight set in the tested range: the five families of Table~\ref{tab:O4} have $\ML$ strictly above the target and $U_1,U_2\ge1$.

\section{Residue and base-point certificates}\label{sec:residue}

\begin{proposition}[Escape lemma]\label{prop:escape}
Let $q\ge1$, $a\in\Z$, $\varepsilon\in\R$, $\delta>0$. If for each $i$ there are integers $N_i,r_i$ with $v_ia=qN_i+r_i$ and $\delta\le r_i/q+v_i\varepsilon\le1-\delta$, then $\delta\le\ML(v)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} At $t=a/q+\varepsilon$ we have $v_it=N_i+r_i/q+v_i\varepsilon$, so $\norm{v_it}\ge\delta$ for all $i$.\end{proof}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over (\texttt{ML\_ge\_of\_escape}). With $\varepsilon=0$ this is the definition of a proper tuple in \cite[Def.~3]{Trak26}, the witness-time ansatz of \cite{Rosenfeld25}; the extension to $\varepsilon\ne0$ is a routine generalization}

Without a bound on $q$ the statement is empty: $\delta\le\ML(v)$ holds if and only if some $(q,a,\varepsilon)$ works, with $q=1$ (\texttt{lonely\_iff\_escape}, VERIFIED, carried over), and for $\delta<\ML(v)$ one can take $\varepsilon=0$ (\texttt{rational\_point\_of\_lt}, VERIFIED, carried over). The window lemma at levels $m+1<q\le2m+2$ and the certificates used in the archive are instances (\texttt{window\_via\_escape}, \texttt{famF\_via\_escape}, \texttt{lemmaP\_via\_escape}). All content lies in the bound on $q$.

\begin{proposition}[Missing divisor]\label{prop:dc}
If $2\le j\le m+1$ and $j$ divides no $v_i$, then $\norm{v_i/j}\ge1/j$ for all $i$, so $\ML(v)\ge1/j$ with witness $t=1/j$. In particular $\ML(v)>1/(m+1)$ if $j\le m$, and $\ML(v)\ge1/(m+1)$ if $j=m+1$.
\end{proposition}
\lab{The bound $\norm{v_i/j}\ge1/j$ is PROVED in one line: $v_i\bmod j\in[1,j-1]$. The Lean lemma \texttt{witness\_of\_missing\_divisor} (VERIFIED, carried over) has the signature $j\le(k+2)\min(r,j-r)$ with $r=v_i\bmod j$ for $k+1=m$ speeds, that is $\norm{v_i/j}\ge1/(m+1)$, the weak form; the strict form is not stated there, and no other part of that Lean statement was re-read in this revision. Known: \cite[Lemma~5.1(i)]{Tao18}; also the survey \cite{PS25} proves that the conjecture holds when some $k\le n+1$ divides no speed, and \cite[Lemma~2.3]{Zhang26} is the case $t=1/r$ for one-entry modifications. Not claimed as new}

Consequently a tight vector satisfies: every $j\in[2,m]$ divides some speed, since otherwise $\ML(v)\ge1/j>1/(m+1)$. This is why the crux is restricted to DC vectors.

\begin{proposition}[Denominators of lonely times]\label{prop:growth}
Let $m\ge2$, $Q\ge1$, $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,Q)$ and $v^{(Q)}=(1,L,2L,\dots,(m-1)L)\in\Z_{>0}^m$. If $t=a/q\in\mathbb Q$ in lowest terms has $\gap_{v^{(Q)}}(t)>0$, then $q>Q$. (b) $\ML(v^{(Q)})\ge1/(m+1)$, witnessed by $t=c/(L(m+1))$ for any integer $c$ with $L\le c\le Lm$ and $c\equiv1\pmod{m+1}$; such $c$ exists. Consequently, for every function $Q(m)$ there is a vector with $m$ speeds that has lonely times at level $1/(m+1)$ but none of denominator at most $Q(m)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $q\le Q$ then $q\mid L$, so $Lt\in\Z$ and $\norm{Lt}=0$. The speed $L$ occurs in $v^{(Q)}$ because $m\ge2$, so $\gap(t)=0$. Hence $q>Q$ whenever $\gap(t)>0$.

(b) The interval $[L,Lm]$ contains $L(m-1)+1$ integers. For $L=1$ it contains $1\equiv1$. For $L\ge2$ it contains at least $m+1$ consecutive integers, hence one with $c\equiv1\pmod{m+1}$. Put $t=c/(L(m+1))$. Since $L\le c\le Lm$, $t\in[\frac1{m+1},\frac m{m+1}]$ and $\norm{t}\ge\frac1{m+1}$ (speed $1$). For $1\le j\le m-1$ the speed $jL$ gives $jLt=jc/(m+1)$; $\gcd(c,m+1)=1$ and $m+1\nmid j$, so $m+1\nmid jc$ and $\norm{jc/(m+1)}\ge\frac1{m+1}$. Hence $\gap(t)\ge\frac1{m+1}$, and by (a) its denominator is $>Q$. For the consequence take $Q=Q(m)$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: a speed divisible by every small denominator is invisible to every small-denominator test, because it sends all rational times of small denominator to $0$ mod $1$. Zoom map: L1.1 reduction of $q\le Q$ to $q\mid L$ (arithmetic, no lemma); L1.2 positivity of the gap forces $q>Q$; L1.3 existence of $c$ and the bound $\gap(t)\ge\frac1{m+1}$ at $t=c/(L(m+1))$. For $Q\ge2$ the subvector $(L,2L,\dots,(m-1)L)$ obtained by deleting the speed $1$ has $\gcd=L>1$, so $v^{(Q)}$ is outside the class of the crux, whose members have primitive leave-one-out subvectors. No prior statement was located in the literature read}

\paragraph{Rationality of maximizers.}
The gap is piecewise linear with slopes $\pm v_i\ne0$, so it has no flat piece and each local maximum is a vertex of its graph; every vertex is the solution of a linear equation with integer coefficients, hence rational. So every maximiser of $\gap_v$ is rational, and for $v=v^{(Q)}$ every maximiser, which has positive gap, has denominator $>Q$ by the proposition. This is not needed for the proposition.

\paragraph{Obstruction for residue-condition methods.} A test at denominator $q$ sees only the residues $v_i\bmod q$ of the speeds, and a speed with residue $0$ at every level up to $Q$ is invisible to all of them; Proposition~\ref{prop:growth} makes this exact, and the families of Table~\ref{tab:O4} realize it approximately inside the class of the crux by a few tall speeds (up to $4943$) whose residues complete a covering of the small levels. The mechanism is that the residues of a few free tall speeds can be chosen independently modulo each small $q$ while the maximal loneliness stays above the target; this explanation is consistent with the table and is not proved, label HEURISTIC.

\subsection{Level drift}\label{sec:drift}
Let $\mathrm{TL}(Q)$ denote the statement: for $m\ge12$ and every class member $v$ of the crux, there are $q\le Q(m)$, a unit $a$ modulo $q$, $\sigma=\pm1$ and $\varepsilon\ge0$ such that Proposition~\ref{prop:escape} applies at $t=\sigma a/q+\varepsilon$ with the first-order arc condition on the non-multiples of $q$ and a condition on the multiples of $q$ through $\varepsilon$. \section{Local corners: the Goddyn--Wong mechanism and statement (T)}\label{sec:corner}

\paragraph{Corners.} $\ML(v)$ is attained at a time $k/D$ with $D\in\{2v_i,v_i+v_j,|v_i-v_j|\}$ and $0\le k<D$ (\cite[Section~2]{PS25}; the numerators are not restricted to residues coprime to $D$). A local argument fixes such a pair and controls the maximum from it; the remaining $m-2$ speeds enter only through their residues modulo $D$. For $\ML(v)<\frac12$ every maximizer is such a corner, of a rising and a falling tent (Proposition~\ref{prop:corner}).

For multipliers $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{n-1})\in\mathbb N^{n-1}$ write $[n-1]_\mu=(\mu_1\cdot1,\dots,\mu_{n-1}(n-1))$, with $n=m+1$. The \emph{GCD condition} is: for each $r\in[n-1]$ with $\mu_r\ge2$ and each $b$ with $n-r\le b\le\mu_r(n-r)-1$, $\gcd(r,b)>1$. Goddyn and Wong prove $\ML([n-1]_\mu)=1/n$ under this condition \cite[Thm.~3.1]{GW06}, quoted as \cite[Thm.~5.1]{Zhang26}; for one accelerated entry it is also necessary \cite[Thm.~2.3]{GW06}, quoted as \cite[Thm.~1.1]{Zhang26}. Zhang classifies all one-entry modifications $[n-1]_{r\to w}$, $w>n-1$: tight if and only if $2r>n-1$, $r\mid w$ and the GCD condition holds with $\mu=w/r$, or $(n,r,w)\in\{(5,2,7),(6,2,9)\}$ \cite[Thm.~1.2]{Zhang26}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:O6}
If $\mu$ satisfies the GCD condition, then $n$ divides no speed $\mu_rr$, $1\le r\le n-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\mu_r=1$ then $0<r<n$. Let $\mu_r\ge2$ and suppose $\mu_r(n-r)\ge n$. The interval $[n-r,\mu_r(n-r)-1]$ has length $\mu_r(n-r)-(n-r)\ge r$, so it contains $r$ consecutive integers, one of which is $\equiv1\pmod r$ and hence coprime to $r$; this contradicts the GCD condition. So $0<\mu_r(n-r)<n$. Now $\mu_rr+\mu_r(n-r)=\mu_rn$, so $n\mid\mu_rr$ would give $n\mid\mu_r(n-r)$, impossible for $0<\mu_r(n-r)<n$.
\end{proof}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over (\texttt{not\_dvd\_speed}; axioms recorded as \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}). A statement of this form was not located in the sources read; the original proof of \cite[Thm.~3.1]{GW06} was not available for this audit and may contain the same step. The published argument obtains a sharper size bound; only the weaker one is used}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:GWDC}
Under the GCD condition with $\mu_1=1$ (forced, since $\gcd(1,b)=1$), the vector $[n-1]_\mu$ is not DC: $j=n=m+1$ divides no speed. The same holds for the vectors $(1,3,4,7)$, $(1,3,4,5,9)$, $(1,4,5,6,7,11,13)$, $(1,2,3,4,5,7,12)$, $[1..11]\cup\{13,24\}$, $[1..17]\cup\{19,36\}$ and for the two-entry doubling instance $[1..73]\setminus\{70,72\}\cup\{140,144\}$ with $n=74$ \cite[Cor.~5.2 and the example following Thm.~5.1]{Zhang26}.
\end{corollary}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over for $[n-1]_\mu$ under the GCD condition (\texttt{not\_divisorComplete}). For the listed vectors: HEURISTIC, an exact computation (script \texttt{code/obstructions/gwcheck.py}) of $\ML=1/n$ and of the non-divisibility, RESOLVED}

\begin{remark}
A vector built by the Goddyn--Wong construction is excluded from the class of the crux, since the proof of its tightness shows $m+1\nmid v_i$. The mechanism uses three inputs: the baseline $[n-1]$, the identity $\mu_rr\equiv-\mu_r(n-r)\pmod n$ valid because the new speed is an integer multiple of an existing entry, and the GCD hypothesis reused through a size bound. A general class member has no such decomposition, and the generalizations in the literature (Zhang's one-entry classification, the multi-swap theorem) keep the multiplicative form. The route therefore gives no lower bound for divisor-complete vectors. The converse of the multi-acceleration theorem is not true in general \cite[Question~9.3]{Zhang26}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Obstruction for local-corner methods.} The maximum of $\gap_v$ is a corner determined by two speeds, but whether the corner is a maximum depends on the residues of every other speed modulo the corner's denominator, and these residues carry no structure that a pair-local argument can use. In the Goddyn--Wong families the missing control is supplied by a global identity, namely that every speed is a multiple of an entry of $[n-1]$ and the sum $\mu_rr+\mu_r(n-r)$ is a multiple of $n$; for a vector without this decomposition the maximizer still sits at denominator $m+1$ in every tight case computed (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}), but no pair of speeds identifies which speed would carry the divisor $m+1$ if it existed. This is why a direct local proof of (T) below gets no purchase, label HEURISTIC.

\subsection{Statement (T)}

(T) $m+1$ divides no $v_i$;
(T$'$) every $t$ with $\gap_v(t)=\ML(v)$ has the form $a/(m+1)$, $a\in\Z$.
\end{conjecture}
\lab{CONJECTURE. Provenance: observation on the tight vectors of Goddyn--Wong type and on exhaustive small boxes. Evidence: Section~\ref{sec:evidence}, exhaustive primitive search for $m=3,\dots,8$ in boxes finds the tight vectors listed there, all satisfying (T) and (T$'$). \begin{proposition}\label{prop:T}
Let $v\in\Z_{>0}^m$ and suppose $m+1\mid v_i$ for some $i$. Then $\gap_v(a/(m+1))=0<\ML(v)$ for every $a\in\Z$. Consequently (T$'$) implies (T), and (T) implies that no tight primitive vector is DC.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $v_i/(m+1)\in\Z$, $v_i\cdot a/(m+1)\in\Z$ and $\gap_v(a/(m+1))\le\norm{v_it}=0$. Also $\ML(v)>0$, as $\gap_v(t)>0$ at any irrational $t$. Assume (T$'$) and let $v$ be primitive and tight with $m+1\mid v_i$. A maximizer $t$ exists, since $\gap_v$ is continuous and $1$-periodic; by (T$'$), $t=a/(m+1)$, so $\gap_v(t)=0\ne\ML(v)$, a contradiction. Hence (T) holds. A DC vector has $m+1\in\{2,\dots,m+1\}$ dividing some $v_i$, which (T) excludes for tight primitive $v$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED (the implications only; neither (T) nor (T$'$) is proved). Idea: a speed divisible by $m+1$ vanishes at every candidate time of denominator $m+1$, so such a time cannot be a maximizer. \begin{proposition}[Tight corner]\label{prop:corner}
Let $v\in\Z_{>0}^m$ with $\ML(v)=a/b<\frac12$ in lowest terms, and let $t^*$ be a maximizer of $\gap_v$. Then $t^*=k/L$ in lowest terms is rational, and there are indices $i,j$ with $v_it^*\equiv a/b$ and $v_jt^*\equiv-a/b\pmod 1$ such that
\[
b\mid L,\qquad L\mid b\gcd(v_i,v_j),\qquad L\mid v_i+v_j .
\]
In particular $L=bd$ with $d\mid\gcd(v_i,v_j)$. If $v$ is tight and $m\ge2$ then $b=m+1$, so (T$'$) holds at $t^*$ if and only if $d=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
L1: (a) at a maximizer some speed sits at $+a/b$ and some at $-a/b$; (b) the two congruences give the divisibilities.

(a) Put $\delta=a/b$ and $S=\{i:\norm{v_it^*}=\delta\}$, nonempty since $\gap_v(t^*)=\delta$. As $0<\delta<\frac12$, each $i\in S$ has $v_it^*\equiv\sigma_i\delta\pmod 1$ with a unique $\sigma_i\in\{+1,-1\}$. Suppose $\sigma_i=+1$ for all $i\in S$. For small $\varepsilon>0$, $\norm{v_i(t^*+\varepsilon)}=\delta+v_i\varepsilon$ for $i\in S$, and $\norm{v_i(t^*+\varepsilon)}>\delta$ for $i\notin S$ by continuity. Then $\gap_v(t^*+\varepsilon)>\delta=\ML(v)$, which is absurd. The case $\sigma_i=-1$ for all $i\in S$ is the same with $t^*-\varepsilon$. So there are $i,j\in S$ with $\sigma_i=+1$, $\sigma_j=-1$. Then $v_it^*=N_i+a/b$ and $v_jt^*=N_j-a/b$ with $N_i,N_j\in\Z$; in particular $t^*\in\mathbb Q$, say $t^*=k/L$ with $\gcd(k,L)=1$.

(b) Adding, $(v_i+v_j)k/L=N_i+N_j\in\Z$, so $L\mid(v_i+v_j)k$ and $L\mid v_i+v_j$. Multiplying $v_ik/L=N_i+a/b$ by $bL$ gives $bv_ik=L(bN_i+a)$. Hence $L\mid bv_ik$, so $L\mid bv_i$; and reducing modulo $b$ gives $b\mid La$, so $b\mid L$ because $\gcd(a,b)=1$. In the same way $bv_jk=L(bN_j-a)$ gives $L\mid bv_j$. Thus $L\mid\gcd(bv_i,bv_j)=b\gcd(v_i,v_j)$, and $L=bd$ gives $d\mid\gcd(v_i,v_j)$.

For tight $v$ and $m\ge2$, $\ML(v)=1/(m+1)\le\frac13<\frac12$, so $a=1$, $b=m+1$. (T$'$) at $t^*$ says $L\mid m+1$; with $m+1\mid L$ this is $L=m+1$, that is $d=1$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: at a maximizer one runner sits exactly at distance $+\ML$ ahead of an integer and another exactly $\ML$ behind (the two residues $\pm\ML$ are distinct because $\ML<\frac12$), otherwise the time could be moved to raise the gap; clearing the denominator of $\ML$ turns the two corner equations into congruences that bound the denominator of the maximizer by $b$ times a common divisor of the corner pair. Zoom map: L1.1 opposite signs at a maximizer; L1.2 divisibilities from the two corner equations. That a maximizer lies at a time $k/D$ with $D$ a sum of two speeds is the vertex lemma \cite[Lemma~2.2]{Cordella26}, \cite[Section~2]{PS25}; the divisibilities $b\mid L$ and $L\mid b\gcd(v_i,v_j)$ were not located in the sources read. For tight $v$ with $m+1\mid v_l$ and $d=1$ at a maximizer, $v_lt^*\in\Z$, which is impossible; so a tight primitive vector with a speed divisible by $m+1$ would have $d\ge2$ at every maximizer. Worked example: $v=(1,2,4,6)$, $\ML=1/4$, $t^*=3/8$, $L=8$, $b=4$, $d=2$, corner pair $(6,2)$ with $6t^*\equiv\frac14$, $2t^*\equiv-\frac14$, $L\mid v_i+v_j=8$, $L\mid b\gcd(6,2)=8$ (not tight: $\ML=1/4>1/5$). So $d>1$ occurs, and the hypothesis $\gcd(v)=1$ is needed for (T$'$). Not formalized}

\section{Doubling pairings}\label{sec:doubling}

In the covering formulation a lonely time is ruled out by exhibiting bad sets that cover the window of integers $[1,2m]$ for the modulus $q=2m+1$; the smallest such sets are pairs. For $q$ odd let $N=\{v\le 2m:\gcd(v,q)>1\}$ be the non-units in the window. The doubling graph on $[1,2m]$ has edges $\{v,2v\}$ with $2v\le 2m$; it lives on the integers of the window, not on residues modulo $q$.

\begin{proposition}[Chain parity]\label{prop:chain}
Let $q=2m+1\ge3$ be odd, $N=\{v\in[1,2m]:\gcd(v,q)>1\}$, and let $\Gamma$ be the graph on $N$ with edges $\{v,2v\}$, $2v\le2m$. If $q$ is composite and $q\ne9$, then $s=q-2p$, with $p$ the least prime factor of $q$, is an isolated vertex of $\Gamma$; in particular $\Gamma$ has no perfect matching. If $q$ is prime then $N=\emptyset$, and if $q=9$ then $N=\{3,6\}$ is a single edge. Hence every component of $\Gamma$ has an even number of vertices if and only if $q$ is prime or $q=9$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $q$ be composite and $p$ its least prime factor. Then $q=pr$ with $r\ge p\ge3$, so $q\ge3p$ and $q\ge p^2$. Put $s=q-2p$. Then $s$ is odd, $p\le s\le q-6\le2m$, and $\gcd(s,q)=\gcd(2p,q)=p>1$, so $s\in N$. Being odd, $s$ has no neighbour $s/2$. Its other possible neighbour $2s$ lies in $[1,2m]$ if and only if $2s\le q-1$, that is $q\le4p-1$. In that case $p^2\le q\le4p-1$ forces $p\le3$, hence $p=3$ and $q\le11$ is an odd composite multiple of $3$, so $q=9$. Thus for $q\ne9$ the vertex $s$ is isolated, a component with one vertex. If $q$ is prime no $v\in[1,2m]$ has $\gcd(v,q)>1$. If $q=9$, $N=\{3,6\}$ and $\Gamma$ is the edge $\{3,6\}$. An isolated vertex is a component of odd size and admits no perfect matching.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: for composite $q>9$ the non-unit $q-2p$ is odd, so it has no halving partner, and its double exceeds the window, so it has no doubling partner; it is an isolated vertex of the doubling graph on the non-units of the window. Zoom map: L1.1 $q-2p$ is a non-unit ($\gcd=p$); L1.2 it is odd; L1.3 its double exceeds $2m$ unless $q=9$. Independent check: brute-force chain lengths (the components of $\Gamma$) for odd $q\le 20001$, no mismatch (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). Not formalized}

\begin{remark}
The companion covering paper classifies every bad pair with non-unit endpoints as a doubling pair or a pair with $a+b\in\{2m+1,2m+2\}$ (Lean-verified for the dichotomy; carried over), and pairs with $a+b=2m+1$ and both endpoints non-units do not occur. For $q\in\{25,27,33,35,49\}$ no non-unit bad pair with $a+b=2m+2$ was found in earlier computations, which are not regenerated here (HEURISTIC). If the same holds for all composite $q$, Proposition~\ref{prop:chain} implies that a partition of the window into bad pairs cannot cover the non-units, so parts of size at least three are forced; this implication is conditional and carries the label HEURISTIC.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Obstruction for doubling methods.} A pairing of the window by $\{j,2j\}$ treats the integers as a union of doubling chains, and a component with an odd number of vertices leaves one vertex unmatched. For a composite modulus $q>9$ the vertex $q-2p$ is an odd non-unit whose double exceeds the window, so it is an isolated vertex of the doubling graph on the non-units; this is the whole mechanism, and it does not depend on any property of the speeds. Passing to the cyclic group $\Z/q\Z$, where doubling is a permutation, does not repair it, since the bad sets of the covering formulation are multiples of $j$ at modulus $j$ and do not realize the wrap-around partner (earlier computations, not regenerated; HEURISTIC).

\section{Covering radius and the zero phase}\label{sec:phase}

For phases $s\in\R^m$ put $\gap_{v,s}(t)=\min_i\norm{s_i+v_it}$. Henze and Malikiosis identify $\ML(v)$ with $\frac12-\frac12\kappa(Z(v))$ and the shifted supremum $\sup_t\gap_{v,s}(t)$, minimized over $s$, with $\frac12-\frac12\mu(Z(v))$, where $\kappa$ is the first minimum and $\mu$ the covering radius of the lonely runner zonotope $Z(v)$ \cite[Prop.~1.7]{BCS26}. Blanco, Criado and Santos show by exact computation that the shifted infimum for $v=(1,\dots,n)$ is below $1/(n+1)$ for $5\le n\le17$ speeds, for instance $15/94<1/6$ for $(1,2,3,4,5)$ \cite[Prop.~5.11, 5.14, Thm.~1.8]{BCS26}; Poliakova shows that the defect grows linearly and is positive for $n\ge95$ \cite[Thm.~1, 2]{Poliak26}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:phase}
There is no $t\in\R$ with $\norm{t}\ge\frac17$, $\norm{2t-\frac12}\ge\frac17$, $\norm{3t-\frac12}\ge\frac17$, $\norm{4t-\frac12}\ge\frac17$, $\norm{6t-\frac12}\ge\frac17$ and $\norm{9t}\ge\frac17$. That is, for speeds $(1,2,3,4,6,9)$ and phases $(0,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,\frac12,0)$ the shifted lonely runner bound for seven runners fails.
\end{proposition}
\lab{VERIFIED, carried over (\texttt{halfShift\_not\_lonely}). Computed value (HEURISTIC, exact): the supremum of the shifted gap is $3/22$, attained at $t=7/22$, while the zero-phase value for the same speeds is $\ML=1/5$ (\texttt{code/obstructions/shift\_grid.py}, exact rational scan of all $t=p/q$, $q\le 400$, and independent recomputation of $\ML$)}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:cr}
By the dictionary above, a bound $\mu(Z(v))\le (n-1)/(n+1)$ for all $v$ with $n$ speeds is false for $5\le n\le17$ (equivalent to the failure of the shifted bound, cited computation, label HEURISTIC as a computation by others, reproduced for one instance by the exact scan above). The covering-radius route of \cite{MSS25}, whose Proposition~2.7 bounds $\mu(C)\le\lambda_1(C-C)+\mu(\pi(C),\pi(\Z^d))$ for a convex body $C$, can therefore be used for the conjecture only through $\kappa$, never as a bound at the target value for $\mu$. \cite[Lemma~2.5]{MSS25} (if $|C\cap\Z^d|>\ell^d$ then $\lambda_1(C-C)\le1/\ell$) is a statement on an arbitrary convex body; its conclusion is unchanged by translating $C$, and the zero phase enters only through the lattice-point count \cite[Cor.~2.3]{MSS25}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Obstruction for covering-radius methods.} Any argument whose steps remain valid when the zero phase is replaced by a phase vector in $\{0,\frac12\}^m$ proves a false statement for $v=(1,2,3,4,6,9)$, so a proof of the conjecture must use the zero phase, that is the common starting point of all runners. The covering radius is a translation-invariant quantity and is the right invariant for the shifted problem only; the target quantity is the first minimum. The mechanism is the failure of the shifted statement, proved above for one explicit instance.

\section{Descent and topological obstructions}\label{sec:descent}

This section records two classes of arguments that do not reach the floor. The first is an induction on the number of speeds that passes from a set to its leave-one-out subsets through an inequality between values of $\ML$. The second is a topological necessary condition for the straight line $t\mapsto tv$ to avoid the cube $[\delta,1-\delta]^m$ in the torus. In this section $V$ is a set of $m$ distinct positive integers, identified with the vector of its elements, and $V\setminus v$ is $V$ without the element $v$. For $m\ge2$ put
\[\loo(V)=\min_{v\in V}\ML(V\setminus v).\]
A \emph{tight leave-one-out} of $V$ is an element $v$ with $\ML(V\setminus v)=1/m$, the value of the floor for $m-1$ speeds. For $k\ge1$ put
\[\sigma(k)=\inf\{\ML(W):\ W\text{ a set of }k\text{ distinct positive integers},\ \ML(W)>1/(k+1)\}.\]
No value of $\ML$ at $k$ speeds lies strictly between $1/(k+1)$ and $\sigma(k)$.

\subsection{Deletion and dilation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:D0}
Let $V$ be a finite multiset of positive integers with at least two elements and let $c\ge1$ be an integer. (a) For every element $v$ of $V$, $\ML(V\setminus v)\ge\ML(V)$. (b) $\ML(cV)=\ML(V)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) For every $t$, $\gap_{V\setminus v}(t)$ is a minimum over a subset of the terms of $\gap_V(t)$, so $\gap_{V\setminus v}(t)\ge\gap_V(t)$; take the maximum over $t$. (b) $\gap_{cV}(t)=\gap_V(ct)$, and $t\mapsto ct$ maps $\R$ onto $\R$, so the two functions have the same range.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: deleting a speed removes a constraint, and dilating all speeds reparametrizes time. Zoom map: L1.1 pointwise inequality of the gaps; L1.2 surjectivity of $t\mapsto ct$. Not formalized (a Lean statement of (b) for tuples exists in the source tree as \texttt{ML\_smul}, see \texttt{LEAN\_MAP.md}; (a) is absent). Elementary; the corresponding deletion statement for the shifted problem is \cite[Lemma~7.14]{Zhang26}, a citation that was not re-checked in this revision}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:D0}
Lemma~\ref{lem:D0} bounds $\ML$ of a subset from below by $\ML$ of the whole set. It does not give $\ML(V\setminus v)\ge1/m$, which is the floor for $m-1$ speeds. In particular it does not show that a subset of a counterexample with $m$ speeds is not a counterexample with $m-1$ speeds; that is the conjecture at $m-1$ speeds applied to the subset.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Leave-one-out descent}

For $m\ge2$ a \emph{descent function at $m$} is a function $F:(0,\tfrac12]\to\R$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DI}
\loo(V)\le F(\ML(V))\qquad\text{for every set }V\text{ of }m\text{ distinct positive integers.}
\end{equation}
It is \emph{sub-floor} if $F(x)<\sigma(m-1)$ for all $0<x<1/(m+1)$. The point of the definition is the following. Suppose the floor holds for $m-1$ speeds and $F$ is a sub-floor descent function at $m$. If $\ML(V)<1/(m+1)$, then some $v$ has $\ML(V\setminus v)<\sigma(m-1)$, hence $\ML(V\setminus v)=1/m$ because $\ML(V\setminus v)\ge1/m$ and no value lies in $(1/m,\sigma(m-1))$. So a sub-floor descent function would send every counterexample with $m$ speeds to a tight set with $m-1$ speeds.

\begin{lemma}[Descent lemma]\label{lem:desc}
If $F$ is a descent function at $m$, then $F(1/(m+1))\ge\loo(V)$ for every tight $V$ with $m$ speeds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:DI} to $V$, for which $\ML(V)=1/(m+1)$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: a descent function is also required to be correct on the tight sets, and there it must be at least as large as their leave-one-out values. Zoom map: L1.1 the defining inequality at $V$. Not formalized. The quantifier is over all sets of $m$ distinct speeds, tight ones included; it is not a statement about all tight sets, only about each one}

\begin{proposition}[Descent discontinuity]\label{prop:nogo}
Let $m\in M$ and let $F$ be a sub-floor descent function at $m$. Then
\[\sup_{0<x<1/(m+1)}F(x)\le\sigma(m-1)<\loo(V_m)\le F\!\left(\tfrac1{m+1}\right).\]
In particular $F$ is not left-continuous at $1/(m+1)$, and its jump there is at least $\loo(V_m)-\sigma(m-1)>0$; at $m=7$ this is at least $1/429$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality is the definition of sub-floor, the second is Observation~\ref{obs:inst}(c), the third is Lemma~\ref{lem:desc} for $V_m$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Class excluded and class not excluded.} The proposition concerns functions of $\ML(V)$ alone that satisfy \eqref{eq:DI} for all sets of $m$ distinct speeds, tight sets included, that stay below $\sigma(m-1)$ under the floor and that are left-continuous at the floor, and it concerns deletion of one speed. It does not exclude: inequalities with structural hypotheses on $V$ (leave-one-out primitivity, divisor-completeness, a given relation); inequalities whose right-hand side uses other invariants of $V$; inequalities with a threshold above $\sigma(m-1)$ followed by a classification of the sets that are near the floor; deletion of two or more speeds (the first two near-tight sets of Observation~\ref{obs:near} contain tight subsets of several smaller sizes); functions with a jump at the floor, which satisfy \eqref{eq:DI} vacuously below the floor if the floor holds for $m$ speeds and carry no information about counterexamples. The left side of \eqref{eq:DI} is itself a value of $\ML$ of subsets; what is excluded is a function of $\ML(V)$ alone with the stated properties.

\paragraph{Conceptual abstract.} The move: to run an induction on the number of speeds, ask for an inequality that sends a hypothetical counterexample with $m$ speeds to a subset with $m-1$ speeds forced to be tight, namely $\min_v\ML(V\setminus v)$ is below the least value above the floor that $m-1$ speeds can take, whenever $\ML(V)$ is below the floor, with a bound that depends on $V$ only through $\ML(V)$ and is continuous at the floor. The exchange: an existence statement becomes an inequality between two numbers. The mechanism that defeats it: some tight sets (Goddyn--Wong sets with one fast speed, and the $m=7$ set $(1,4,5,6,7,11,13)$) have every leave-one-out value strictly above that value, so the bound must jump exactly at the floor. Left open: inequalities that look at lonely times, or at structure, or delete two speeds.

\subsection{Topology of the bad region}\label{sec:topology}

Let $\mathbb T^m=\R^m/\Z^m$ and $q:\R^m\to\mathbb T^m$ the quotient map. For $0<\delta\le\frac12$ put
\[U_\delta=\{x\in\mathbb T^m:\ \norm{x_i}<\delta\text{ for some }i\},\qquad C_\delta=\mathbb T^m\setminus U_\delta=q([\delta,1-\delta]^m).\]
$U_\delta$ is the union of the $m$ open slabs around the coordinate subtori; $C_\delta$ is one closed cube (a point if $\delta=\frac12$) in the open fundamental cube $q((0,1)^m)$. For $v\in\Z^m$ let $\gamma_v:\R/\Z\to\mathbb T^m$, $\gamma_v(t)=q(tv)$. Then $t\in G_\delta$ if and only if $\gamma_v(t)\in C_\delta$, so $\ML(v)\ge\delta$ if and only if $\gamma_v$ meets $C_\delta$.

\begin{proposition}[Bad region]\label{prop:bad}
Let $0<\delta\le\frac12$ and let $i:U_\delta\to\mathbb T^m$ be the inclusion.\\
(a) For $m\ge2$, $i_*:H_1(U_\delta;\Z)\to H_1(\mathbb T^m;\Z)=\Z^m$ is an isomorphism.\\
(b) For $m\ge3$, $i_*:\pi_1(U_\delta,x_0)\to\pi_1(\mathbb T^m,x_0)$ is an isomorphism for every $x_0\in U_\delta$. For $m=2$ and every $x_0\in U_\delta$ it is surjective and not injective.\\
(c) For $m=1$, $U_\delta$ is an open arc and $i_*$ on $H_1$ is not an isomorphism.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $c=(\frac12,\dots,\frac12)$, $\rho(x)=\norm{x-c}_\infty$ for $x\in[0,1]^m$, and identify $(0,1)^m$ with its image under $q$. Then $C_\delta=\{\rho\le\frac12-\delta\}$. Put $B=\{x\in(0,1)^m:\rho(x)<\frac12-\frac\delta2\}$, an open cube. Since $\delta>0$, $C_\delta\subset B$, so $\mathbb T^m=U_\delta\cup B$, and $U_\delta\cap B=\{\frac12-\delta<\rho<\frac12-\frac\delta2\}$. Since $\rho>\frac12-\delta\ge0$ on $U_\delta\cap B$, $c\notin U_\delta\cap B$, and $x\mapsto\bigl((x-c)/\rho(x),\rho(x)\bigr)$ is a homeomorphism of $U_\delta\cap B$ onto $S\times(\frac12-\delta,\frac12-\frac\delta2)$, where $S=\{y:\norm{y}_\infty=1\}\cong S^{m-1}$. The set $U_\delta$ is path connected: each slab is connected and contains $0$. The set $B$ is contractible.

(a) and (b), $m\ge3$. $S^{m-1}$ is connected and has $H_1=0$ and $\pi_1=1$. The Mayer--Vietoris sequence of the open cover $\{U_\delta,B\}$ contains
\[H_1(U_\delta\cap B)\to H_1(U_\delta)\oplus H_1(B)\to H_1(\mathbb T^m)\to\widetilde H_0(U_\delta\cap B),\]
in which $H_1(U_\delta\cap B)=H_1(S^{m-1})=0$, $\widetilde H_0(U_\delta\cap B)=0$ and $H_1(B)=0$; the middle map is $i_*$ on the first summand. Hence $i_*$ is an isomorphism on $H_1$. By the van Kampen theorem for the open path-connected sets $U_\delta$, $B$ with path-connected intersection, $\pi_1(\mathbb T^m)$ is the amalgam of $\pi_1(U_\delta)$ and $\pi_1(B)=1$ over $\pi_1(U_\delta\cap B)=1$, that is $\pi_1(U_\delta)$, the identification being induced by $i$. Path connectedness of $U_\delta$ makes the statement independent of the base point.

(a) and (b), $m=2$. Let $\lambda(x)=2\rho(x)\in(0,1]$ on $[0,1]^2\setminus\{c\}$ and $h_s(x)=c+(1-s+s/\lambda(x))(x-c)$, $s\in[0,1]$. Then $\lambda(h_s(x))=(1-s)\lambda(x)+s\ge\lambda(x)$, $h_s$ is the identity on $\partial[0,1]^2$ (where $\lambda=1$), and $h_1$ maps into $\partial[0,1]^2$. Since $U_\delta=q(\{\lambda>1-2\delta\})$ and $q$ identifies only points of $\partial[0,1]^2$, $h_s$ induces a strong deformation retraction of $U_\delta$ onto $Z=q(\partial[0,1]^2)=\{x_1=0\}\cup\{x_2=0\}\subset U_\delta$. The set $Z$ is a wedge of two circles $a_1=\{x_2=0\}$, $a_2=\{x_1=0\}$; $H_1(Z)=\Z^2$ with basis $[a_1],[a_2]$, and $\pi_1(Z)$ is free on $a_1,a_2$. The loops $a_1,a_2$ represent the standard generators of $H_1(\mathbb T^2)=\pi_1(\mathbb T^2)=\Z^2$. So $i_*$ is an isomorphism on $H_1$. On $\pi_1(U_\delta,0)=F(a_1,a_2)$ it is the abelianization $F_2\to\Z^2$, hence surjective, and not injective because the commutator of $a_1,a_2$ is nontrivial in $F_2$; for another base point $x_0$ use a path in $U_\delta$ from $0$ to $x_0$.

(c) $\mathbb T^1=\R/\Z$ and $U_\delta=q((-\delta,\delta))$ is an arc, $H_1(U_\delta)=0\ne\Z=H_1(\mathbb T^1)$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Hypotheses used: $\delta>0$ for $C_\delta\subset B$; $\frac12-\delta\ge0$ so that the shell $U_\delta\cap B$ is bounded away from the centre $c$ ($\delta<\frac12$ is not needed: the statement and proof hold for $0<\delta\le\frac12$, and for $\delta>\frac12$ the set $U_\delta$ is the whole torus); $m\ge2$ for the connectedness of the shell; $m\ge3$ for its simple connectivity Prior art: removing a ball from a manifold of dimension at least $3$ does not change $\pi_1$, and from a torus of dimension at least $2$ does not change $H_1$; this is standard, and no novelty is claimed for it or for its application here; no literature search was made for the lonely runner formulation}

\paragraph{Conceptual abstract.} The central idea is to ask what the inclusion of the bad region into the torus can detect about a speed vector. The bad region, where some runner is within $\delta$ of the origin, is the torus minus one cube; the sphere around the cube is connected, and simply connected from dimension three, so the inclusion is an isomorphism on first homology in dimension at least two and on the fundamental group in dimension at least three. Hence the curve traced by a speed vector is homotopic into the bad region, as lifting also shows. For curves symmetric under negation the one remaining obstruction is the midpoint, a fixed point in the cube exactly when all speeds are odd; otherwise half the curve is a path between fixed points, pushed in by lifting. So such conditions detect only the all-odd vectors, where the maximum is already $\frac12$.

\paragraph{Zoom map for Propositions~\ref{prop:bad} and~\ref{prop:equiv}.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[L1.1] The bad region is the torus minus one closed cube $C_\delta$ (definition of $U_\delta$; $C_\delta\subset B$, $U_\delta\cap B\cong S^{m-1}\times$ interval).
\item[L1.2] The inclusion $U_\delta\to\mathbb T^m$ is an isomorphism on $H_1$ for $m\ge2$ and on $\pi_1$ for $m\ge3$, and is onto on $\pi_1$ for $m=2$ (Mayer--Vietoris for $m\ge3$; van Kampen for $m\ge3$; deformation retraction onto the wedge of two circles for $m=2$).
\item[L1.3] The only obstruction to an equivariant push of $\gamma_v$ into $U_\delta$ is the position of $\gamma_v(\frac12)=v/2\bmod1$, which equals $p$ if and only if all $v_i$ are odd (equivariant loops are paths between fixed points; path lifting in $\R^m$, one coordinate at a time, with another coordinate at an integer).
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:vac}
Let $m\ge2$, $v\in\Z^m$ and $0<\delta\le\frac12$. There is a loop in $U_\delta$ based at $0$ that is homotopic to $\gamma_v$ in $\mathbb T^m$ rel the base point $0$; in particular $[\gamma_v]\in\operatorname{im}(i_*:H_1(U_\delta)\to H_1(\mathbb T^m))$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\gamma_v$ is the image under $q$ of the segment from $0$ to $v$. Let $\sigma$ be the path in $\R^m$ that first moves $x_1$ from $0$ to $v_1$, with $x_2=0$, and then moves $x_k$ from $0$ to $v_k$ for $k=2,\dots,m$ in turn, with $x_1=v_1\in\Z$. Every point of $\sigma$ has a coordinate that is an integer, so $q\circ\sigma$ lies in $U_\delta$; the first leg uses $m\ge2$. The straight-line homotopy in $\R^m$ from $\sigma$ to the segment fixes the endpoints $0$ and $v$, and its image under $q$ is a homotopy rel $0$ in $\mathbb T^m$ from $q\circ\sigma$ to $\gamma_v$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. The proof does not use Proposition~\ref{prop:bad}; it uses $\delta>0$ and $m\ge2$ only (for $m=1$ and $v=2$ the statement fails, as $U_\delta$ is an arc). Positivity and primitivity of $v$ are not used and not claimed}

An \emph{equivariant loop} is a loop $\gamma:\R/\Z\to\mathbb T^m$ with $\gamma(-t)=-\gamma(t)$; an \emph{equivariant homotopy} is a homotopy through equivariant loops. The loop $\gamma_v$ is equivariant. The fixed points of $x\mapsto-x$ on $\mathbb T^m$ are the $2^m$ points of $(\frac12\Z/\Z)^m$; exactly one of them, $p=(\frac12,\dots,\frac12)$, lies in $C_\delta$.

\begin{proposition}[Parity]\label{prop:equiv}
Let $m\ge2$, $0<\delta\le\frac12$ and $v\in\Z^m$. The loop $\gamma_v$ is equivariantly homotopic to an equivariant loop with image in $U_\delta$ if and only if not all $v_i$ are odd.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $F$ be the set of fixed points of $x\mapsto-x$, a finite set. An equivariant loop $\gamma$ has $\gamma(0),\gamma(\frac12)\in F$, because $t=0$ and $t=\frac12$ are fixed by $t\mapsto-t$, so equivariance gives $\gamma(t)=-\gamma(t)$ there. During an equivariant homotopy these two points move continuously in the finite set $F$, so they are constant.

If all $v_i$ are odd, then $\gamma_v(\frac12)=p\notin U_\delta$, so every equivariantly homotopic loop passes through $p$.

Suppose not all $v_i$ are odd, and choose $i$ with $v_i$ even. Then $b=\gamma_v(\frac12)=v/2\bmod 1$ has $i$-th coordinate $0$, so $b\in F\setminus\{p\}$ and $b\in U_\delta$. Let $\beta=\gamma_v|_{[0,1/2]}$, the image under $q$ of the segment from $0$ to $v/2$. Let $\sigma$ be the path in $\R^m$ that first moves $x_i$ from $0$ to $v_i/2\in\Z$, with another coordinate $x_j=0$ (this uses $m\ge2$), and then moves each other coordinate $x_k$ from $0$ to $v_k/2$ in turn, with $x_i\in\Z$. Every point of $\sigma$ has a coordinate that is an integer, so $\beta'=q\circ\sigma$ lies in $U_\delta$, and the straight-line homotopy in $\R^m$ from $\sigma$ to the segment, composed with $q$, is a homotopy rel endpoints in $\mathbb T^m$ from $\beta$ to $\beta'$; let $H:[0,\frac12]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb T^m$ be it, $H(\cdot,0)=\beta$, $H(\cdot,1)=\beta'$. Define $\gamma_s(t)=H(t,s)$ for $t\in[0,\frac12]$ and $\gamma_s(t)=-H(-t,s)$ for $t\in[-\frac12,0]$. The two definitions agree at $t=0$ ($H(0,s)=0=-0$) and at $t=\pm\frac12$ ($-b=b$), so each $\gamma_s$ is an equivariant loop, $\gamma_0=\gamma_v$, and the image of $\gamma_1$ is $\beta'([0,\frac12])\cup-\beta'([0,\frac12])\subset U_\delta$, since $U_\delta=-U_\delta$.
\end{proof}
\lab{PROVED. Idea: an equivariant loop is a path between two fixed points, and fixed points cannot move; a path between two fixed points in $\mathbb T^m$ is lifted to $\R^m$, where it can be replaced by a path through the slabs that is homotopic to it rel endpoints. Zoom map: L1.1 equivariant loops correspond to paths between fixed points; L1.2 the endpoint $\gamma_v(\frac12)$ is $p$ exactly for all $v_i$ odd; L1.3 path lifting in $\R^m$. The proof does not use Proposition~\ref{prop:bad} or Corollary~\ref{cor:vac}. Hypotheses used: $m\ge2$ in the first leg of $\sigma$ (fails for $m=1$, $v=2$); $\delta\le\frac12$ only for $p\notin U_\delta$ and for $p$ being the only fixed point in $C_\delta$; positivity, distinctness, primitivity and gcd of $v$ are not used and not claimed If all $v_i$ are odd then $\ML(v)=\frac12$ (the parity trick). The same argument for phase shifts in $\{0,\frac12\}^m$ is not stated here}

\begin{remark}[What the topology excludes]\label{rem:topo}
Corollary~\ref{cor:vac} and Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv} say that a necessary condition for $\gamma_v\subset U_\delta$ obtained from the inclusion $U_\delta\to\mathbb T^m$ (membership of $[\gamma_v]$ in the image of $H_1$ or $\pi_1$, or the equivariant version) holds for every $v$ with not all $v_i$ odd, so such a condition never yields a contradiction with $\ML(v)<\delta$. The statement is about arguments of this form. It is not the statement that no function of the class $[\gamma_v]=v$ distinguishes $\ML(v)\ge\delta$ from $\ML(v)<\delta$: $\ML$ is a function of $v$. What is absent from the topological data is the straight-line parametrization of $\gamma_v$. For $v$ with all $v_i$ odd the equivariant condition fails, and $\ML(v)=\frac12\ge\delta$, so no contradiction is available there either. No statement is made here about degrees, Euler classes or other invariants. Label: HEURISTIC, as this delimits a method; the propositions it rests on are PROVED.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Parity and the value $\frac12$]\label{rem:twoadic}
For $v\in\Z_{>0}^m$, $\ML(v)=\frac12$ if and only if all $v_i$ have the same $2$-adic valuation. Proof. If $v_i=2^ku_i$ with $u_i$ odd for all $i$, then at $t=2^{-k-1}$ every $v_it=u_i/2$ has $\norm{v_it}=\frac12$. Conversely, if $\ML(v)=\frac12$, a time $t$ with $\norm{v_it}=\frac12$ for all $i$ satisfies $v_it\in\frac12+\Z$, so $t=r/s$ in lowest terms and $2v_ir=(2a_i+1)s$ with $a_i\in\Z$; comparing $2$-adic valuations, $1+\nu_2(v_i)+\nu_2(r)=\nu_2(s)$ for every $i$, so $\nu_2(v_i)$ does not depend on $i$. The parity condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv} is not invariant under $v\mapsto cv$, while $\ML$ is (Lemma~\ref{lem:D0}(b)); there is no conflict, since Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv} concerns one necessary condition. Example: $\ML(2,6)=\frac12$. Label: PROVED (two lines; checked by the author only; also computed with the standard routine for $200$ random vectors, $104$ with $\ML=\frac12$, no mismatch).
\end{remark}

\section{Open statements}\label{sec:open}

\begin{itemize}
\item (T) and (T$'$), Conjecture~\ref{conj:T}. Evidence: the exhaustive boxes above and every tight vector of Goddyn--Wong type (Corollary~\ref{cor:GWDC}). Consequence: (T) is equivalent to the statement that no tight primitive vector is DC (Conjecture~\ref{conj:T} and Proposition~\ref{prop:dc}); (T$'$) implies (T) (Proposition~\ref{prop:T}).
\item (T2), Conjecture~\ref{conj:T2}. Evidence: equality $2/(2m+1)$ is the minimum on DC vectors in the boxes for $m=3,\dots,7$. Consequence: strict floor for primitive vectors with $(m+1)\mid v_i$.
\item (C1), Conjecture~\ref{conj:C1}. Evidence: the $59$ known tight vectors without a tight leave-one-out. Consequence: every descent function at $m$ is at least $2/(2m-1)$ at the floor wherever such a vector exists.
\end{itemize}
Whether the class of the crux contains a tight vector is open. None of the three statements is used elsewhere in this note.
