## Abstract

For positive integer speeds `v_1,...,v_m` let `ML(v) = sup_t min_i dist(v_i t, Z)`. The lonely runner conjecture (LRC) is
`ML(v) >= 1/(m+1)` for all `v`. This archive contains seven manuscripts and the material behind their computed statements:
(1) the inequality for speeds in `[1,2m]`, formalized in Lean 4 for every `m` with `2m+1` prime and for all 73 values of `m <= 126`
with `2m+1` composite (so for every `2 <= m <= 126`), including `m = 94` by a tree of three pinned cover-capacity certificates; (2) a covering framework for bounded velocity windows with a classification of the small bad sets;
(3) a catalogue of obstructions to certificate-based proofs for divisor-complete families, with a section on leave-one-out descent and on the topology of the bad region; (4) a covering bound for
modifications of the tight instance `[1,n-1]` and its consequences; (5) a Lean-checked reduction of LRC for all `m` to
three finite-range statements and one remaining statement (`FloorCruxU`) that is equivalent to LRC given the other three;
(6) a Lean formalization of the case of eight runners, with a theorem that depends on the standard axioms only, compiled outside the lake build; (7) exact leave-one-out values of tight Goddyn-Wong vectors in three regimes, with written proofs that rest on two quoted results and are not formalized. Since version 1.2.0 the archive also contains (8) a Markdown note with a hand proof, and a Lean proof, of a special case of a known theorem (four moving runners, phases in `(1/5)Z`; see the entry for version 1.2.0 below). Since version 1.3.0 it also contains (9) a second Markdown note, with a computation-free proof and a Lean proof of a sub-case of a known theorem (four moving runners, arbitrary real phases, some speed divisible by 5; see the entry for version 1.3.0 below). No proof of LRC is claimed.

## Idea in words

Every statement here rests on one fact: `ML(v) >= delta` holds as soon as some time `t` has `dist(v_i t, Z) >= delta` for all `i`.
A velocity set with no such time must contain, for every time, a velocity whose phase is within `delta` of an integer, that is it must
hit the "bad set" of that time. For a window `[1,2m]` and `delta = 1/(m+1)` there are finitely many distinct bad sets, so the inequality for the
whole window is a finite hitting-set statement: no `m` velocities hit all bad sets. Such a statement is certified by a
fractional weighting of times (weak LP duality), by a cover of the window by parts with capacities, or by exhaustive search. The
Lean files check the certificates with the kernel. The other manuscripts study the limits of this method (barrier theorem, level drift),
a measure argument for single and multiple modifications of `[1,n-1]`, the reduction to one crux, and the case of eight runners.

## Statement of significance

Tao's 2017 blog post describes the inequality `ML >= 1/(m+1)` for speeds in `[1,1.2m]` (proved in the accompanying preprint arXiv:1701.02048, as described in the post) and names the range `[1,2m]` as an interesting sub-problem. To our knowledge no paper proves it for all sets of `m` distinct speeds in `[1,2m]`, either in general or for an infinite family of `m`; Theorem 3 below does this for all `m` with `2m+1` prime, and the table of the dense-window manuscript with the case `m = 94` for all `m <= 126`. Basis: a search of 9 November 2026 (web search and arXiv abstract pages; full text read for Sungkawichai-Trakulthongchai arXiv:2604.23906 and Beck-Everett arXiv:2609.06259; Tao's blog post read in part; the statements on `[1,1.2m]` and `[1,2m]` are taken from it and were not checked in the preprint) covering Tao, the survey of Perarnau and Serra (arXiv:2409.20160), Bedert (arXiv:2511.16636), Sungkawichai-Trakulthongchai (including their Proposition 1.4, which prescribes residues modulo a large prime and not the size of the speeds), Zhang (arXiv:2608.13599), Beck-Everett, Rosenfeld (arXiv:2509.14111) and Blanco-Criado-Santos (arXiv:2603.24784). The papers of Bohman and Peng, Pomerance and Pandey were read afterwards, on 2026-11-09 (arXiv version 1 for the first two; for Bohman and Peng the abstract, Section 1, Theorem 2.1 with proof and Section 3, the number-theoretic lemma proofs and small-n casework only skimmed, the published Mathematika text not read), and none states the window for any `m`. The result is a special case of the lonely runner conjecture, by a single author, and has not been refereed. A search that finds nothing is not a proof of novelty.

## Idea in words, obstruction note

The note tests seven standard routes to the floor at tight vectors. A fixed integrable weight cannot certify a tight vector: its good set is countable and without mass. Bounded-denominator certificates fail without a leave-one-out hypothesis, because a speed divisible by `lcm(1,...,Q)` vanishes at every time of denominator at most `Q`. Doubling pairings fail because, for odd composite `2m+1` above 9, the non-unit `2m+1-2p`, with `p` the least prime factor, has no partner in the window. Corner analysis fails because a corner maximum depends on the residues of all other speeds. Covering-radius bounds fail because the shifted statement is false. Descent through leave-one-out subsets fails because some tight vectors have every leave-one-out value above the next value attained by `m-1` speeds. Topological conditions fail because the bad region has the first homology of the torus. What remains is an explicit rational time or a classification of tight vectors.
